Chapter1 - Technical Skills Don't Guarantee Influence

Created2026-02-27|Updated2026-09-05|Communication
|Post Views:
  1. Why people overlook your ideas?
  2. Why they interrupt you?
  3. Why they listen to others advice even when they repeat exactly what you said?

  • Many people growing up were rewarded for their technical ability not their delivery, not communication.

  • At school, at university, even on your career, you are granted for your knowledge and technical ability, not your communication , delivery

  • So we subconsciously developed those believe. If the idea is good enough, delivery shouldn’t matter. The idea should speak for itself.

  • People were hard to listen to you , They wont believe in what you say

  • Even if you are technically brilliant, say you’re a 10 out of 10 with your technical skills, you’re a genius.But if your communication skills are just 3 out of 10, people will find it hard to listen to you.

overlook

to fail to notice or consider something or someone

  • I think there is one key fact that you have overlooked.
  • No one will be overlooked when we select players for the team.

subconsciously

the part of your mind that notices and remembers information when you are not actively trying to do so, and influences your behaviour even though you do not realize it

  • I think I must have known subconsciously that something was going on between them
  • Sometimes people subconsciously want to get caught.

During our time at school, if we had spent more time developing soft skills—instead of focusing so heavily on knowledge and technical training—we would have gained a more diverse and well-balanced set of abilities.

And maybe it’s not just “lack of communication skills.” It’s the awareness of that lack.

Because when you feel safe relying on grades, scores, and knowledge, you might not practice the skills that create real-world confidence:

  • speaking under pressure
  • handling disagreement
  • presenting ideas with authority
  • adapting to the audience

So later, when communication becomes the bottleneck, you feel anxious—not because you’re unprepared technically, but because you’re missing the delivery muscles you never had to train.
